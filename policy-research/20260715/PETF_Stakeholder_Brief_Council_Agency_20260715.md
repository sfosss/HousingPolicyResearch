# PETF Stakeholder Brief — July 15, 2026
## For: NYC Council Housing Committee · HPD · HRA · NYCHA · Mayor's Office
**From:** Mamdani Transition Housing Policy Team
**Re:** Four Market & Legal Developments That Harden the Case for the Public Equity Transfer Framework

### The Core Argument in One Sentence
Today's data confirms what we already knew structurally: NYC's rental market is in a fiscal emergency, the legal floor for tenants is in place but not being held, and continuing to funnel public dollars into rent-assistance without building tenant equity is both economically irrational and morally indefensible.

---

### Development 1 — Good Cause Eviction Creates the Legal Floor; PETF Builds the Ceiling

New York State's Good Cause Eviction Law (L. 2024, ch. 56) is now in full effect, capping evictions and limiting rent increases to roughly the lesser of 5% + CPI or 10% annually. This is the most significant statutory tenant protection in a generation.

**What this means for PETF:** The law creates stable tenure conditions — precisely the prerequisite for equity transfer to be viable and investable. Tenants who cannot be displaced arbitrarily can accumulate equity without fear of losing it. PETF converts Good Cause's defensive floor into an offensive wealth-building mechanism. Without PETF, Good Cause stabilizes poverty. With PETF, it enables ownership.

**Ask of Council:** Codify Good Cause non-preemption language into the PETF enabling bill (Amendment AM-2026-001).

---

### Development 2 — Evictions Rising, Legal Defense Collapsing: The Status-Quo Subsidy System Is Failing

The NYC Comptroller's June 2026 report *Evictions Up, Representation Down* documents a dangerous convergence: eviction filings are rebounding toward 180,000 annualized cases, while Right to Counsel representation has dropped from a peak of ~65% to approximately 44–48% of Housing Court cases. The RTC program — the city's primary eviction-prevention tool — is being defunded in real time.

**What this means for PETF:** This is not a patch problem; it is a structural failure. A system that depends on annual legal defense budgets to prevent displacement is inherently fragile. PETF eliminates the underlying displacement risk by converting tenants from renters (who can be litigated out) into equity-holders (who have ownership standing). The fiscal ROI of PETF compounds as RTC costs continue to rise.

**Data point for Council:** At an average RTC case cost of ~$3,000–$5,000 and 74,000+ represented cases in 2025, the city spent $220M–$370M on legal defense alone — with no asset formation for the tenant.

**Ask of Council:** Mandate an RTC Coverage Gate in PETF Phase II expansion criteria (Amendment AM-2026-003).

---

### Development 3 — Rents at DefCon 1: Voucher Expansion at These Prices Is Fiscally Irrational

As of July 2026, average Manhattan 1-bedroom rents have crossed $5,050/month; Brooklyn is at $4,300; Queens at $3,200 — up 8–14% year-over-year across all boroughs. These are all-time highs.

**The math:** A Section 8 voucher for a Brooklyn 1BR now costs the city and federal government approximately $43,000–$51,600 per year in subsidy. Over 15 years, that is $650,000–$775,000 per household — with zero equity accrual. Under PETF, the same payment stream builds a tenant equity stake estimated at 25–35% of a comparable ownership unit's purchase price over the same period, generating a transferable, intergenerational asset.

**What this means for agencies:** HPD and HRA cannot expand voucher programs fast enough to keep pace with $5,000/month rents. The only fiscally sustainable path at these price levels is equity conversion, not subsidy escalation.

**Ask of agencies:** Authorize HPD to immediately update PETF cost models with 2026 rent baselines (Amendment AM-2026-005) and present recalibrated ROI projections at the next Council fiscal briefing.

### Development 4 — The Economic Mobility Summit Validates PETF's Frame; Stake a Claim Today

The NYC Economic Mobility Summit (Center for an Urban Future / Citi, July 15, 2026) is convening city officials, philanthropies, and practitioners around a single thesis: affordable housing must be a platform for economic mobility and wealth-building, not just shelter stabilization. This is PETF's exact thesis.

**Strategic opportunity:** The Summit's conveners and speakers are potential evaluation partners, narrative amplifiers, and philanthropic co-investors for PETF pilots. Being absent from this conversation lets others define the frame. Engaging today positions PETF as the *implementation answer* to the Summit's *diagnostic question*.

**Immediate action:** Assign a Transition Team lead to engage Center for an Urban Future and Citi program staff within 72 hours to explore evaluation partnership and pilot co-funding.
