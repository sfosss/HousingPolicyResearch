## Amendment Set 1 — Good Cause Non-Preemption (Implementation Tier: CRITICAL)

### Instrument: PETF Master Bill (Local Law) — Article I, General Provisions

**Insert new §1-07 (after existing §1-06 Definitions):**

> **§ 1-07 Preservation of Tenant Protections — Good Cause Eviction.**
>
> (a) *Non-preemption.* Nothing in this local law, nor any Equity Transfer Agreement, PETF Participation Agreement, HDFC conversion plan, or any other instrument executed pursuant to or in furtherance of this local law, shall be construed to limit, waive, diminish, supersede, or otherwise abrogate any right, protection, or remedy afforded to tenants under:
>    1. The New York State Good Cause Eviction Law (L. 2024, ch. 56, as amended), including but not limited to protections against non-renewal, eviction without good cause, and unconscionable rent increases;
>    2. The New York City Rent Stabilization Law and Code, as administered by DHCR;
>    3. The Emergency Tenant Protection Act of 1974, as amended; or
>    4. Any successor statute, local law, or regulation providing equivalent or greater tenant protections.
>
> (b) *Enhancement obligation.* The administering agency (HPD) shall ensure that all PETF program instruments — including Equity Transfer Agreements, co-operative conversion plans, HDFC bylaws, and tenant equity account agreements — affirmatively preserve and, where practicable, enhance tenants' security of tenure and rent-regulation rights during conversion and in perpetuity thereafter.
>
> (c) *Know-Your-Rights requirement.* HPD shall cause to be prepared, in all languages required by Local Law 30 of 2017, a plain-language summary of all tenant protections preserved under this section and shall make such summaries available:
>    1. To each household participating in PETF intake;
>    2. As a required rider to each Equity Transfer Agreement; and
>    3. On the HPD public website in an accessible digital format.
>
> (d) *Conflict.* In the event of any conflict or ambiguity between this local law and any applicable state or local tenant-protection statute, the provision affording greater protection to the tenant shall prevail.

## Amendment Set 2 — Eviction-Risk Baselines & RTC Contingency (Implementation Tier: HIGH)

### Instrument A: PETF Fiscal Impact Statement — Section IV, Risk Register & Baseline Assumptions

**[REDLINE — REPLACE existing Paragraph IV-3 Eviction Baseline with the following:]**

> **IV-3. Eviction-Risk Baseline — Updated July 2026.**
>
> *Prior text (struck):* ~~NYC eviction filings are assumed to track 2022–2023 post-moratorium levels of approximately 90,000–140,000 annual filings citywide, with Right to Counsel (RTC) coverage assumed at approximately 65 percent of Housing Court cases.~~
>
> *Revised text (inserted):* NYC eviction filings are projected to reach approximately 155,000–180,000 annualized cases in 2025–2026, while RTC representation has declined to approximately 44–48 percent of Housing Court cases, reflecting funding shortfalls identified in the NYC Comptroller's June 2026 report *Evictions Up, Representation Down* (NYC Comptroller, June 2026). The fiscal model shall use:
>    - **Base case:** 160,000 annual filings citywide; 48% RTC coverage.
>    - **Stress case:** 180,000 annual filings; 40% RTC coverage.
>    - **Optimistic case:** 130,000 annual filings; 65% RTC coverage (contingent on full RTC funding restoration).
>
> The fiscal ROI calculation for avoided shelter and court costs shall apply sensitivity tables across all three scenarios and report the range of net present value (NPV) outcomes.

**[REDLINE — ADD new Paragraph IV-4 after IV-3:]**

> **IV-4. Right to Counsel Contingency Standard.**
>
> PETF pilot expansion milestones shall include a RTC Coverage Gate: expansion from Pilot Phase I (≤500 units) to Phase II (up to 2,500 units) shall be contingent upon RTC representation in PETF-targeted zip codes meeting or exceeding 60 percent of Housing Court cases in the preceding 12-month period, as reported by the Office of Civil Justice. Where coverage falls below this threshold, HPD shall:
>    1. Provide supplemental legal support to PETF participating households through the Universal Access to Counsel program or an equivalent mechanism; and
>    2. Notify the City Council Housing Committee within 30 days, with a remediation plan.

### Instrument B: PETF Implementation Manual — Chapter 3, Tenant Eligibility & Enrollment

**[REDLINE — ADD to §3.4 Tenant Enrollment Requirements:]**

> §3.4(g) *Legal Status Confirmation.* Prior to executing any Equity Transfer Agreement, the PETF program manager shall confirm that the tenant has received:
>    1. A Good Cause Eviction Know-Your-Rights packet in the tenant's primary language (per §1-07(c) of the enabling local law);
>    2. A referral to a RTC-eligible attorney or housing legal services provider; and
>    3. A written disclosure that participation in PETF does not waive any tenant's rights under applicable rent-regulation or eviction-protection laws.

## Amendment Set 4 — Economic Mobility Framing Insert (Implementation Tier: CONSIDERATIONS → MEDIUM)

### Instrument: PETF Master Report — Section 2, Findings & Framework Justification

**[REDLINE — ADD new paragraph at end of §2.3 Policy Rationale:]**

> The PETF is directly aligned with the emerging citywide consensus, evidenced by the NYC Economic Mobility Summit (Center for an Urban Future / Citi, July 15, 2026), that affordable housing must function as a platform for intergenerational wealth-building and economic mobility — not merely a cost-management mechanism. The Summit's convening of city officials, philanthropy, and practitioners to examine housing-as-economic-mobility further validates PETF's core thesis that converting subsidy flows into tenant equity accounts produces superior fiscal and social ROI compared to indefinite rental-assistance dependence. HPD and HRA should engage Summit co-conveners as evaluation partners and potential sources of philanthropic match funding for PETF pilots.
