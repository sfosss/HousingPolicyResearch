## R2 — Local Law A, § 26-1807(a), new paragraph (6)
Existing paragraph (5): “(5) Anti-speculation provisions preventing windfall profits.”
[ADD after paragraph (5): (6) A requirement that termination or non-renewal of a non-purchasing tenant's tenancy be based on a lawful ground stated in the lease and regulatory agreement, with written notice of the ground and an opportunity to contest the termination or non-renewal in a court of competent jurisdiction. Each lease and the recorded affordability covenant shall incorporate this requirement. Nothing in this paragraph shall authorize termination on a ground prohibited by applicable law or waive any rent-regulation, conversion, succession, or other tenant protection.]
redline_note: R2 | Add § 26-1807(a)(6) requiring cause-based tenancy protections in leases and recorded covenants; preserve greater existing protections. Do not represent this clause as establishing a state-law exemption.
Review gate: Counsel must define permissible grounds and cure procedures and confirm duration, enforcement, successor obligations, and consistency with existing regulatory agreements.

## R3 — Local Law A, § 26-1809(b), replacement
[DELETE: (b) During conversion processes, all tenants shall retain their rights under applicable rent regulation, lease protections, and anti-harassment laws.]
[ADD: (b) During conversion processes, all tenants shall retain their rights under applicable rent regulation, lease protections, and anti-harassment laws, including article 6-A of the real property law where applicable. Such disclosure shall supplement, and shall not replace, any notice required by state or local law.]
redline_note: R3 | Replace § 26-1809(b) with an express preservation clause and supplemental lease disclosure; leave statutory notice periods and § 26-1904 purchase-response periods unchanged.

## R4 — Local Law A, § 26-1805, new subdivision (c)
Existing subdivision (b): “(b) The department may adjust these allocations based on property-specific needs, provided that tenant households maintain stable housing and do not experience increased out-of-pocket costs.”
[ADD after subdivision (b): (c) Upon request by a participating household or cooperative concerning an alleged arrear attributable to an agency payment or allocation delay, the department shall, within 10 business days, provide a written statement identifying the payment period, amount due, amount paid, and any unresolved eligibility or processing issue within its records. Where the department confirms that a payment delay is attributable solely to agency administration and the household has timely paid its required contribution, the cooperative shall not impose a late charge, commence a proceeding, or seek termination or non-renewal based solely on that delay while the agency resolves the confirmed payment issue. This subdivision shall not deem an unapproved payment authorized, create an entitlement to assistance contrary to governing program law, or alter the jurisdiction of a court. The department shall establish by rule a correction and dispute-review procedure.]
redline_note: R4 | Add § 26-1805(c): 10-business-day payment accounting and a program-condition protection for confirmed agency-only delays; preserve lawful eligibility determinations and judicial authority. Replaces the source tracker's broader assertion that delay cannot constitute tenant nonpayment.
Review gate: Counsel must review enforceability, unresolved disputes, remedy and funding continuity; verify agency administrative capacity. The 10-business-day deadline is proposed policy, not an existing legal requirement.
