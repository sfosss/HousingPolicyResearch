## R2 — Local Law A, § 26-1807(a), new paragraph (6)
Existing paragraph (5): “(5) Anti-speculation provisions preventing windfall profits.”
[ADD after paragraph (5): (6) A requirement that termination or non-renewal of a non-purchasing tenant's tenancy be based on a lawful ground stated in the lease and regulatory agreement, with written notice of the ground and an opportunity to contest the termination or non-renewal in a court of competent jurisdiction. Each lease and the recorded affordability covenant shall incorporate this requirement. Nothing in this paragraph shall authorize termination on a ground prohibited by applicable law or waive any rent-regulation, conversion, succession, or other tenant protection. Expiration of a lease, refusal to purchase cooperative shares, or an owner's desire to recover a unit for personal or family occupancy shall not constitute cause under the program covenant. A lease or regulatory agreement shall not establish a ground for termination or non-renewal that diminishes any protection applicable to the tenancy under state or local law. Greater protections, and rights that continue after expiration of the covenant, are preserved.]
redline_note: R2 | Critical. Add § 26-1807(a)(6) requiring cause-based tenancy protections in leases and recorded covenants, with notice and opportunity to contest; expiration, refusal to buy shares, and owner personal or family occupancy are not cause; no lease or agreement may diminish state or local protections; preserve greater existing and continuing protections. Do not represent this clause as establishing a state-law exemption.
Review gate: Counsel must define permissible grounds and cure procedures and confirm local authority, enforcement, duration, successor obligations, and consistency with existing regulatory agreements. No state-law exemption is claimed.

## R3 — Local Law A, § 26-1809(b), replacement
[DELETE: (b) During conversion processes, all tenants shall retain their rights under applicable rent regulation, lease protections, and anti-harassment laws.]
[ADD: (b) During conversion processes, all tenants shall retain their rights under applicable rent regulation, lease protections, and anti-harassment laws, including article 6-A of the real property law where applicable. Before execution or renewal of a lease for a non-purchasing tenant, the cooperative shall provide a written disclosure identifying the laws and regulatory agreements governing the tenancy and describing the cause-based termination and non-renewal protections required by this chapter. Such disclosure shall supplement, and shall not replace, any notice required by state or local law. The disclosure shall state whether rents are regulated and identify the applicable rent-setting or rent-increase restrictions. No disclosure provided under this chapter shall be treated as satisfying an exemption from a state notice requirement unless the conditions of that exemption are independently met.]
redline_note: R3 | High. Replace § 26-1809(b) with an express preservation clause and supplemental lease disclosure that states whether rents are regulated and identifies rent-setting or rent-increase restrictions; no disclosure satisfies a state notice exemption unless its conditions are independently met; leave state notice periods and § 26-1904 purchase-response periods unchanged.
Review gate: Verify current RPL §§ 226-c and 231-c and governing notice forms before final drafting; do not import an unverified 100-day period.

## R4 — Local Law A, § 26-1805, new subdivision (c)
Existing subdivision (b): “(b) The department may adjust these allocations based on property-specific needs, provided that tenant households maintain stable housing and do not experience increased out-of-pocket costs.”
[ADD after subdivision (b): (c) Upon request by a participating household or cooperative concerning an alleged arrear attributable to an agency payment or allocation delay, the department shall, within 10 business days, provide a written statement identifying the payment period, amount due, amount paid, and any unresolved eligibility or processing issue within its records. Where the department confirms that a payment delay is attributable solely to agency administration and the household has timely paid its required contribution, the cooperative shall not impose a late charge or rely on that delay, whether individually or as part of an alleged pattern of chronic nonpayment, as a ground for commencing a proceeding or seeking termination or non-renewal. Such prohibition shall continue after resolution of the payment delay and shall be incorporated into the regulatory agreement and each affected lease. This subdivision shall not deem an unapproved payment authorized, create an entitlement to assistance contrary to governing program law, or alter the jurisdiction of a court. The department shall establish by rule a correction and dispute-review procedure.]
redline_note: R4 | Critical (elevated from High). Add § 26-1805(c): 10-business-day payment accounting and a durable program-condition protection, incorporated into the regulatory agreement and leases, against late charges and reliance on confirmed agency-only delays, including as part of an alleged chronic-nonpayment pattern; preserve lawful eligibility determinations and judicial authority. Replaces the source tracker's broader assertion that delay cannot constitute tenant nonpayment.
Review gate: Counsel must review enforceability, disputed or unconfirmed delays, mixed arrears, agency nonresponse, emergency remedies, cooperative cash flow, unresolved disputes, and funding continuity; verify agency administrative capacity. The 10-business-day deadline is proposed policy, not an existing legal requirement.
