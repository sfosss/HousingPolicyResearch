# NYC Housing Subsidy Reform

## Updated Legislative Draft from Repo Materials

### Public Equity Transfer (PET) / Tenant Co-op Acquisition Program (TCAP)

### Draft date: 2026-04-10

> This draft updates the repo-based package by incorporating the legally stronger revisions already reflected in the Mamdani redraft: voluntary-sale purchase rights instead of coercive opt-out triggers, an appropriation-safe revolving fund, HUD carrying-charge guardrails, an AEP-based distressed-building pathway, and a public-land shared-equity annex. It also adds mixed-tenure protections and clearer savings / severability language.

## BILL PACKAGE OVERVIEW

This package is organized as four coordinated local laws and one state enabling track:

1. **Local Law A** — PET/TCAP Program Creation and Governance

1. **Local Law C** — Tenant Purchase Opportunities and Distressed-Building Transfer Pathways
1. **Local Law D** — Public-Land Shared-Equity Annex
1. **State Enabling Track** — PETF statute and conforming amendments to cooperative / property law

# LOCAL LAW A

## A Local Law to amend the administrative code of the city of New York, in relation to establishing the Public Equity Transfer framework and the Tenant Co-op Acquisition Program.

### Section 1. Legislative findings and intent.

The council finds and declares that:

1. The city annually expends substantial public funds on tenant-based and project-linked housing assistance, including but not limited to Housing Choice Voucher assistance, CityFHEPS, HASA housing assistance, state voucher assistance, and related emergency housing expenditures, yet such public expenditure generally produces no tenant equity and limited durable public asset creation.
1. The concentration of subsidy-assisted households in chronically distressed buildings has, in many cases, coincided with recurring code violations, harassment, displacement pressure, and repeated public enforcement costs.
1. The city has a substantial interest in converting a lawful portion of existing housing assistance expenditures into long-term affordability, tenant stability, and collective asset formation, where such conversion is authorized by law and structured in a manner consistent with federal, state, and local requirements.
1. It is therefore city policy to establish a Public Equity Transfer framework and a Tenant Co-op Acquisition Program to support tenant-controlled limited-equity cooperative and community land trust structures, while preserving legal compliance, tenant choice, due process, and affordability protections.

### Section 2. Definitions.

For purposes of this chapter:

**"Carrying charge"** means a monthly amount payable by or on behalf of an occupant-member of a cooperative and attributable only to such occupant-member's proportionate share of permitted blanket mortgage debt service, building operations, insurance, utilities where included, required reserves, regulatory compliance costs, and lawful ground lease obligations.

**"Certified tenant association"** means a tenant association recognized by the department pursuant to rules promulgated under this chapter and demonstrating support from at least 33 percent of occupied units, or such higher threshold as the commissioner may require by rule for acquisition approval.

**"Community land trust" or "CLT"** means a nonprofit entity holding land for long-term community benefit and subject to affordability and governance restrictions approved by the department.

**"Convertible subsidy stream"** means a city, state, or federal housing assistance stream that may lawfully be applied to cooperative carrying charges, occupancy charges, or related affordability supports under applicable law, rule, waiver, or demonstration authority.

**"Department"** means the department of housing preservation and development.

**"Eligible property"** means a residential building designated by the department pursuant to this chapter after consideration of subsidy concentration, physical distress, financial feasibility, tenant organizing readiness, title and lien conditions, and neighborhood stabilization priorities.

**"MOPT"** means the mayor's office to protect tenants.

**"Persistently distressed building"** means an eligible property that meets criteria established by rule, which shall include repeated housing maintenance violations, repeated or unresolved AEP inclusion or comparable enforcement intervention, unpaid emergency repair charges or municipal liens where applicable, or documented harassment patterns.

**"Subsidized limited-equity cooperative" or "SLEC"** means a limited-equity cooperative approved by the department and subject to regulatory restrictions governing affordability, resale, governance, occupancy, and public funding compliance.

### Section 3. Program establishment.

1. The department shall establish and administer the Tenant Co-op Acquisition Program.
1. The program shall support the acquisition, rehabilitation, capitalization, and regulatory oversight of eligible properties to be transferred to a certified tenant association, a subsidized limited-equity cooperative, a CLT-affiliated entity, or another mission-controlled entity approved by the department.
1. The program shall be designed to:
   - convert lawful housing assistance flows into durable affordability and collective equity where permitted;
   - reduce recurring shelter, eviction, and enforcement costs;
   - prioritize historically excluded communities and heavily burdened neighborhoods; and
   - preserve tenant choice, including the right not to purchase shares.

### Section 4. Agency roles and interagency coordination.

1. HPD shall serve as lead program administrator.
1. HRA/DSS shall administer and certify city-controlled subsidy participation consistent with applicable law.
1. NYCHA shall participate where NYCHA-administered vouchers or public housing conversions are implicated.
1. MOPT shall serve as intake, referral, and complaint aggregation partner.
1. OMB shall participate in fiscal review and savings certification under local law B.
1. The corporation counsel and any other necessary agencies may participate through memoranda of understanding.
1. The department shall promulgate rules establishing an interagency operating protocol, including data-sharing, privacy safeguards, eligibility review, pilot selection, and escalation procedures.

### Section 5. Eligibility and selection.

The department shall adopt by rule a transparent scoring framework considering, at minimum:

1. the percentage of subsidy-assisted households in the building;
1. building distress indicators, including code violations, AEP history, harassment findings, and municipal charges;
1. organizing readiness and tenant association capacity;
1. feasibility of acquisition and rehabilitation financing;
1. title, lien, and litigation complexity;
1. long-term affordability and neighborhood stabilization value; and
1. consistency with fair housing and anti-displacement goals.

### Section 6. Tenant choice and mixed-tenure protections.

1. No lawful occupant shall be required to purchase a cooperative share as a condition of continued occupancy.
1. A tenant who does not elect to become a member of the cooperative may remain a protected rental occupant pursuant to a regulatory lease, occupancy agreement, or other department-approved instrument.
1. Where a building contains rent-stabilized tenants at the time of approval, the department shall require a transition plan preserving lawful protections until such protections are superseded by a knowing and voluntary election into an approved ownership structure.
1. The department shall require language access, disability accommodation, succession-rights disclosure, grievance procedures, and due-process protections by rule.

### Section 7. Rulemaking.

Within 180 days of the effective date, the department shall promulgate rules to implement this chapter, including application forms, certification procedures, mixed-tenure safeguards, affordability covenants, reporting requirements, and enforcement standards.

### Section 8. Severability.

If any provision of this chapter or its application is held invalid or preempted, such invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect without the invalid provision.

# LOCAL LAW B

## A Local Law to amend the administrative code of the city of New York, in relation to establishing the Tenant Opportunity Fund and related fiscal reporting.

### Section 1. Establishment of fund.

1. There is hereby established a **Tenant Opportunity Fund** as a special revenue account or other city fund structure authorized by law.
1. Expenditures from the fund shall remain subject to appropriation and all otherwise applicable budget and procurement requirements.

### Section 2. Permitted uses.

Fund monies may be used for:

1. acquisition support and subordinate financing;
1. rehabilitation and capital stabilization;
1. legal and technical assistance;
1. predevelopment costs, title review, appraisal, due diligence, and environmental review;
1. reserve seeding;
1. accessibility and code-compliance work;
1. anti-retaliation support and emergency tenant protections during conversion;
1. data, monitoring, and evaluation; and
1. other program purposes approved by rule.

### Section 3. Capitalization.

Subject to appropriation, the fund may be capitalized by:

1. city appropriations;
1. grants, gifts, settlements, or philanthropic support lawfully accepted by the city;
1. loan repayments and recoveries;
1. revenues from city-authorized financing participation;
1. such portion of documented savings as may be appropriated by the mayor and council following certification under section 4; and
1. other lawful sources.

### Section 4. Fiscal analysis and savings certification.

1. HPD, HRA/DSS, and OMB shall jointly publish, at least biennially, a PET/TCAP fiscal impact report.
1. Such report shall estimate, using stated assumptions, changes in:
   - shelter entry and shelter-cost exposure;
   - eviction-related city expenditures;
   - repeated code-enforcement and emergency repair expenditures;
   - subsidy churn and administrative costs; and
   - long-term affordability preservation value.
1. The report may recommend an amount of documented or reasonably projected savings for appropriation into the Tenant Opportunity Fund.
1. Nothing in this section shall create an automatic entitlement, sweep, or transfer not otherwise authorized through the budget process.

### Section 5. Budget-neutrality standard.

The department shall, to the maximum extent practicable, phase implementation so that each program cohort is designed to be budget-neutral or savings-generating over a ten-year horizon, subject to pilot-phase start-up costs and available appropriations.

### Section 6. Reserve and default protections.

The department shall require reserve policies, recapture provisions where appropriate, and default-management terms sufficient to protect public funds, maintain building habitability, and preserve affordability.

### Section 7. Reporting.

An annual public report shall summarize appropriations, commitments, expenditures, recoveries, fund balance, and project-level obligations, except where confidentiality is required by law.

### Section 8. Severability.

Severability standard identical to Local Law A.

# LOCAL LAW C

## A Local Law to amend the administrative code of the city of New York, in relation to tenant purchase opportunities, distressed-building review, and anti-retaliation protections under the Tenant Co-op Acquisition Program.

### Section 1. Notice of voluntary sale.

1. An owner of an eligible property that intends to enter into a voluntary sale transaction shall provide written notice to the department, MOPT, and any certified tenant association no later than 90 days before closing, or within such alternate time as may be provided by rule for good cause.
1. The notice shall include material sale terms, property condition disclosures required by rule, and information sufficient to permit department review.

### Section 2. Right of first opportunity.

1. Upon a qualifying voluntary sale notice, a certified tenant association, directly or through a department-approved nonprofit, CLT, or cooperative formation entity, shall have a right of first opportunity to submit an offer.
1. If the owner thereafter enters into a bona fide third-party contract, such tenant-side entity shall have a right of first refusal to match substantially equivalent terms within the time period established by rule.
1. This section shall not be construed to:
   - condition an owner's lawful withdrawal from a federal or state subsidy program on a forced sale;
   - require sale at an administratively set appraised value rather than bona fide transaction terms; or
   - prohibit express preemption carve-outs required by federal or state law.

### Section 3. Distressed Building PET Review.

1. The department may initiate a **Distressed Building PET Review** for any persistently distressed building.
1. Before designation, the department shall provide notice and an opportunity to respond.
1. The review shall evaluate:
   - building conditions and enforcement history;
   - municipal liens, tax arrears, mortgage conditions, and title issues;
   - cost of continued status-quo enforcement versus acquisition / transfer;
   - tenant readiness and need for interim stabilization; and
   - feasibility of transfer to a tenant-controlled or mission-controlled structure.
1. Following review, the department may pursue one or more of the following:
   - negotiated sale facilitation;
   - financing and rehabilitation support;
   - transfer through existing lawful city enforcement or disposition mechanisms;
   - temporary stewardship by a mission-controlled entity pending final tenant transfer; or
   - such other lawful remedy as may be available.
1. Nothing herein shall itself create eminent domain authority or override existing state-law foreclosure, lien-priority, bankruptcy, or due-process requirements.

### Section 4. Anti-retaliation.

1. No owner, managing agent, or other person shall retaliate against a tenant for:
   - participating in PET/TCAP organizing;
   - communicating with HPD, MOPT, HRA, NYCHA, legal services, or a certified TA provider;
   - seeking designation, review, or acquisition under this chapter; or
   - exercising rights provided by this chapter.
1. Retaliatory conduct includes unlawful eviction attempts, service reduction, coercion, intimidation, threats, discriminatory treatment, or harassment as otherwise defined by applicable law.
1. The department may refer retaliation matters to appropriate enforcement agencies and may prioritize affected properties for emergency support.
1. This section supplements, and does not limit, any right or remedy available under existing anti-harassment, anti-retaliation, fair housing, or human rights law.

### Section 5. Due process for public designations.

If the department publishes any registry, risk category, or public list related to persistently distressed buildings under this chapter, it shall provide notice, criteria, and an opportunity for administrative correction or appeal pursuant to rules.

### Section 6. Tenant transition safeguards.

1. Any acquisition plan submitted under this chapter shall include:
   - treatment of non-purchasing households;
   - interim lease and occupancy rights;
   - treatment of arrears and repayment policies;
   - language access plan;
   - disability accommodation procedures;
   - succession and inheritance rules; and
   - internal grievance and dispute-resolution procedures.
1. The department shall not approve final transfer absent a determination that such plan is adequate.

### Section 7. Private enforcement.

The council may provide, by further local law or rule where authorized, a private right of action or administrative enforcement process for violations of sections 1 through 4. Pending such action, nothing in this section shall impair existing private remedies otherwise available under law.

### Section 8. Severability.

Severability standard identical to Local Law A.

# LOCAL LAW D

## A Local Law to amend the administrative code of the city of New York, in relation to applying shared-equity principles to city-financed or city-controlled housing development.

### Section 1. Public-land shared-equity policy.

It shall be city policy, where lawful and feasible, to evaluate limited-equity cooperative, CLT-based, or comparable shared-equity tenure structures for city-financed or city-controlled residential developments on public land or long-term ground leasehold interests.

### Section 2. Applicability.

This chapter applies to new construction or substantial rehabilitation projects receiving city capital support, subsidy support, or long-term land control, where ongoing occupancy subsidies are expected for a material share of units and where the project is not primarily designed for a population whose housing model requires a different structure, including certain supportive or transitional housing.

### Section 3. Evaluation requirement.

The sponsoring agency shall evaluate whether shared-equity tenure is feasible and appropriate, considering:

1. affordability preservation;
1. resident stability;
1. financing compatibility;
1. operating capacity;
1. resident governance readiness; and
1. consistency with fair housing and accessibility obligations.

### Section 4. Equity ladder pilots.

The department may establish pilot programs allowing eligible residents in covered projects, after a period of good standing defined by rule, to elect participation in a shared-equity structure through lawful redirection of subsidy support, carrying charges, or capitalized occupancy rights, subject to applicable federal and state law.

### Section 5. Regulatory agreements.

Projects proceeding under this chapter shall include regulatory agreements addressing affordability duration, resale controls, governance, reserve standards, displacement protections, and enforcement rights.

### Section 6. Rulemaking and annual report.

The department shall promulgate rules and annually report on project evaluations, pilot determinations, and barriers encountered, including state or federal changes needed for expansion.

### Section 7. Severability.

Severability standard identical to Local Law A.

# STATE ENABLING TRACK

## Drafting directions for Albany package

The city package should be paired with a state enabling bill that:

1. recognizes **subsidized limited-equity cooperatives** as a distinct regulatory category;
1. authorizes the use of designated subsidy streams as lawful cooperative capitalization or carrying-charge support where not otherwise prohibited;
1. clarifies treatment of restricted equity under state-supervised public assistance rules;
1. establishes or clarifies authority for affordability covenants, resale limits, and inheritance rules in SLECs;
1. provides any necessary Martin Act / offering-plan relief or tailored disclosure regime for low-income tenant-initiated limited-equity conversions; and
1. coordinates with real property, housing, and social services law.

# SAVINGS CLAUSE FOR ENTIRE PACKAGE

Nothing in this legislative package shall be construed to:

1. authorize the use of federal funds in a manner prohibited by federal law or regulation;
1. override state-law lien-priority, foreclosure, bankruptcy, corporate, or securities requirements except where separately amended by state law;
1. diminish existing tenant protections unless a tenant knowingly and voluntarily elects a lawful alternative structure approved by the department; or
1. require any agency to act beyond its lawful authority.
