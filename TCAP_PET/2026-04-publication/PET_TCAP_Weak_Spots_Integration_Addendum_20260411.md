# PET/TCAP Weak-Spots Integration Addendum

## Targeted Revisions for Mayoral / Council Package

**Date:** April 11, 2026\
**Status:** Counsel-review addendum

This addendum integrates five targeted revisions into the April 11 submission package: (1) HUD positioning, (2) state enabling precision, (3) distressed-building transfer mechanics, (4) Martin Act / Attorney General treatment, and (5) agency-validated fiscal assumptions.

## 1. HUD positioning

### Revision

The package should state explicitly that the pilot launch phase is limited to currently authorized cooperative carrying-charge treatment and does not assume that existing law already authorizes direct voucher-funded equity credits, down payments, or individual purchase-loan amortization.

### Insertable narrative

Under current federal law, TCAP's near-term Housing Choice Voucher pathway is limited to what HUD already permits. Voucher assistance may support cooperative carrying charges under 24 C.F.R. § 982.619, with the cooperative treated as the owner and the assisted household treated as the member-occupant whose assistance covers its proportionate share of debt service, operating costs, and reserve contributions. During the pilot phase, TCAP should operate inside that existing carrying-charge framework only. Any move from carrying-charge support to a formally recognized equity-credit mechanism should be treated as a second-phase federal request, to be pursued through available administrative flexibility, Moving to Work tools where applicable, homeownership-option modifications, or, if necessary, targeted statutory change.

### Insertable compliance clause

The department shall implement this chapter in two phases with respect to federal Housing Choice Voucher participation. In the initial phase, any voucher assistance applied in a subsidized limited-equity cooperative shall be treated solely as cooperative carrying-charge support consistent with 24 C.F.R. § 982.619, and no portion shall be booked as a down payment or as amortization of an individual family purchase loan. Concurrently, the department, HRA/DSS, and any participating public housing authority may seek such administrative flexibility, demonstration authority, homeownership-option modifications, or statutory change as may be necessary to authorize a defined share of assistance to be recorded as restricted member equity, subject to all otherwise applicable federal eligibility and asset rules.

## 2. State enabling precision

### Revision

The package should stop implying that the City can create the full legal category on its own. The correct posture is that the City package depends on parallel state enabling legislation in the affordable-housing and cooperative-law framework.

### Insertable narrative

The City package should not be read as a self-contained rewrite of New York cooperative and property law. The contemplated structure requires state enabling legislation that works through, rather than around, the existing affordable-housing and cooperative-law framework. The preferred approach is to amend the Private Housing Finance Law and the Cooperative Corporations Law to recognize a regulated category of subsidy-backed limited-equity cooperatives, with final article placement and numbering assigned by Albany legislative counsel.

### Replacement sentence

Replace any residual reference to specific Real Property Law sections as the primary enabling path with: "State enabling legislation amending the Private Housing Finance Law and the Cooperative Corporations Law to recognize subsidized limited-equity cooperatives and authorize subsidy-backed affordability and equity-accounting mechanisms consistent with this framework."

## 3. Distressed-building transfer mechanics

### Revision

The package should explain that the enforcement-to-ownership pathway is not automatic and should be triggered only after a structured review comparing the cost of continued enforcement against acquisition and transfer.

### Insertable narrative

TCAP's enforcement-to-ownership pathway is reserved for persistently distressed properties where status quo enforcement has failed. A building should enter Distressed Building PET Review only after it meets threshold distress indicators established by rule, such as repeated AEP cycling, chronic Class B and C violations, significant unresolved municipal charges, or comparable evidence of persistent noncompliance. Once that threshold and the program's subsidy-concentration and tenant-readiness criteria are met, HPD should convene a Distressed Building PET Review to compare two ten-year cost curves: continued enforcement, emergency repair, litigation, and shelter exposure under the status quo; and acquisition, rehabilitation, and regulated transfer under TCAP. Where the TCAP scenario is cheaper or budget-neutral and legally feasible, transfer to a tenant-controlled or mission-controlled entity should become the preferred remedy, using whatever existing lawful tool is available, including negotiated sale facilitation, third-party transfer mechanisms, receivership-related pathways, or interim stewardship arrangements.

### Insertable code clause

Where a distressed-building public equity transfer review determines that acquisition and conversion under this chapter is feasible and is projected to result in fiscal savings or budget neutrality, the department shall, to the extent permitted by law and program rules, prioritize transfer of such property to a certified tenant association, subsidized limited-equity cooperative, community land trust, or other mission-controlled entity rather than to a for-profit third party.

## 4. Martin Act and Attorney General oversight

### Revision

The package should expressly acknowledge Attorney General jurisdiction over cooperative offerings and conversions and should not imply that the City can waive or narrow Martin Act requirements by local law.

### Insertable narrative

All TCAP conversions should proceed in coordination with the Attorney General's existing authority over cooperative offerings and rental-building conversions. Under current New York law, cooperative interests and offering plans are regulated by the Attorney General's Real Estate Finance Bureau under Article 23-A of the General Business Law and related regulations. The state enabling track should therefore contemplate either a simplified affordability-focused review path for government-supervised limited-equity conversions or express authority for the Attorney General to provide streamlined treatment by regulation, guidance, or no-action practice. In all cases, the City should not claim independent authority to waive Martin Act or offering-plan requirements.

### Insertable state companion sentence

This act shall be implemented in coordination with the Office of the Attorney General, which may promulgate regulations or issue determinations providing simplified offering-plan or disclosure treatment for government-supervised subsidized limited-equity cooperative conversions that meet strict affordability, resale, and governance requirements, consistent with Article 23-A of the General Business Law.

## 5. Agency-validated fiscal assumptions

### Revision

The package should make clear that its numbers are rooted in public benchmarks but remain internal modeled outputs unless and until validated by HPD, OMB, and, where appropriate, IBO.

### Insertable narrative

The package's fiscal model should be presented as benchmarked but not yet agency-certified. The per-unit acquisition and rehabilitation band is anchored in public affordable-housing program experience and related market comparators, while the recurring subsidy and shelter comparators are drawn from publicly reported city and state data. The resulting TCAP cost, equity, and break-even figures are therefore best read as internal modeled outputs using public benchmarks, not as official City savings estimates. Early implementation should require HPD and OMB to publish a validation note updating or revising those assumptions using live city expenditure data.

### Insertable governance clause

Within twelve months after enactment, the TCAP Task Force, in coordination with HPD analytics staff and the Office of Management and Budget, and after consultation with any other appropriate fiscal office, shall publish a validation memorandum updating the program's per-unit capital assumptions, operating assumptions, and projected savings or budget-neutrality estimates using current city expenditure data.
