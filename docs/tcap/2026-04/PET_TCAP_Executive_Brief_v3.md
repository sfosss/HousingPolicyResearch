## Problem

New York City spends more than $4 billion per year on rental subsidies and shelter operations that produce no permanent housing asset and no household wealth. CityFHEPS averages $21,154 per household per year; HCV averages $16,896 per household per year; family shelter averages $84,800 per family per year. None of these dollars create a terminal exit point from public dependency, and none build durable equity for the families they serve. At the same time, an estimated 80,000 multifamily units across the five boroughs are in physical or financial distress, creating a once-in-a-generation acquisition opportunity that the City's existing tools (TPT, ANCP, Article XI) are too slow and undercapitalized to capture.

## The PET / TCAP Solution

The Public Equity Transfer (PET) Framework redirects a portion of existing subsidy spending — at the moment of building distress — into the acquisition of those buildings and their transfer to limited-equity cooperatives owned by current tenants. The Tenant–Cooperative Acquisition Program (TCAP) is the operational vehicle, executed by HPD and HDC under four companion Council bills (INT 2026-A through D). Each acquired building is rehabilitated, capitalized through a blended HPD/HDC/LIHTC stack, and converted to a tenant-owned LEHC. Members accrue equity tracking the pro-rata share of blanket mortgage amortization, fully consistent with 24 CFR §982.619.

## Why Now — Mamdani Alignment

The incoming administration's housing pillar under EO 08 commits the City to durable, asset-creating interventions over recurring private-landlord transfers. PET is the framework principle that operationalizes that commitment without requiring the elimination of any existing assistance program. TCAP is the first concrete pilot vehicle. The legislative window in the 2026 Council session, the unusually large pipeline of distressed buildings, and the near-term fiscal pressure on shelter spending all argue for action in FY27 rather than later.

## Fiscal Case (Corrected)

| Program               | Annual cost / HH | Permanent equity created |
| --------------------- | ---------------: | -----------------------: |
| Shelter (family)      |          $84,800 |                       $0 |
| CityFHEPS             |          $21,154 |                       $0 |
| HCV (NYCHA)           |          $16,896 |                       $0 |
| **TCAP (stabilized)** |     **~$21,800** |             **~$66,500** |

**Honest framing.** TCAP costs approximately 3% more per household per year than CityFHEPS during the stabilization period. In exchange, it creates ~$66,500 in permanent household equity per family, where CityFHEPS creates none. After Year 12–13, TCAP's net operating cost per household drops below CityFHEPS as debt service amortizes.

**Per-unit capital investment.** $128,000–$200,000, midpoint **$165,000**, validated against the HPD ANCP cost band.

**ROI.**

- *Direct asset-creation ROI:* **30.8%** — for each public dollar invested above the CityFHEPS baseline, $0.308 of permanent public/cooperative equity is created.
- *Societal ROI (30 years):* **6.2:1** — inclusive of avoided shelter, avoided ER utilization, reduced displacement turnover, and stabilized property tax base. Reported separately and not aggregated with the direct figure.

**Pilot scale (1,000 units).** $165M capital investment; ~$21.8M/yr operating subsidy through Year 12; ~$66.5M permanent equity created.

**Conservative posture.** This brief uses the bottom of every range and presents the societal ROI separately from the direct ROI. It does **not** repeat the prior "$37.5B savings" claim, which the April 2026 Fiscal Audit could not substantiate.

## Legal Feasibility

The April 2026 Legal Consistency Review identified seven issues. All seven have been addressed in the bill text incorporated into the companion proposal:

1. **COPA (INT 2026-A)** narrowed to *voluntary sales only*, with explicit carve-outs for federal/state program exits and a savings clause.
1. **20% equity credit (INT 2026-B)** reframed as the cooperator's pro-rata share of blanket mortgage amortization, aligning with **24 CFR §982.619**.
1. **Revolving fund (INT 2026-D)** structured as *subject to appropriation*; "receipts-limited" language confined to the revolving capital component.
1. **Mitchell-Lama** and active HPD regulatory agreement buildings expressly exempted from compulsory triggers.
1. **DA referral language** uniformly **permissive** ("may refer").
1. **Charter §227** Borough Board notice pathway preserved.
1. **Severability** and standard ULURP/§197-c compliance preserved.

Residual risks (the *Mother Zion* line on COPA, future HUD interpretive guidance) are real but mitigated. The Law Department is asked to issue a confirming opinion before the first closing.
